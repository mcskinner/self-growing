# Collective learning

Status: exploratory; no reporting service or shared registry implemented.

## From family tree to shared experience

The [journal](sources/self-growing-journal.md) imagines forks reporting lessons about growing software and exchanging “genes” through a library rather than necessarily peer to peer. Its Portiid/hive-mind analogy points toward accumulated, reusable knowledge—not a settled networking or governance design.

The potentially useful shared material is more than code: [adaptation descriptions](adaptations-and-capability-exchange.md), application histories, failures, repairs, and observations about which transformations transfer well.

## Sharing need not mean surveillance

There are different possibilities:

- Instances automatically report activity or lessons.
- Owners deliberately publish selected, reviewed adaptations and application evidence.
- Recipients share only [locally distilled improvement candidates](federated-hardening.md).

Discussion favored deliberate, reviewed contributions as a safer initial direction. It did not establish an architecture or consent protocol. Local apps may contain private documents, personal preferences, and incidental complexity; these are not automatically useful collective knowledge.

## Evidence and authority

“Worked for one person” and “reliably transfers” are different claims. A library should not turn repeated assertions into apparent validation. Keeping candidate suggestions, evidence, and acceptance decisions separate could help, but the policy remains open.

Centralized discovery and decentralized authorship can coexist. A registry might eventually help people find material, but it is not necessary to test the present file-based loop.

## Questions

How are useful contributions discovered, attributed, and revised? What happens when lessons conflict? Who chooses the regression cases? How does provenance survive contextual reimplementation? How can an individual withdraw or correct shared evidence?
